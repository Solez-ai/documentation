<Note>
  HollowRover operates as the physical execution layer of the LifeSphere ecosystem — the only subsystem capable of acting on the world rather than merely sensing it.
</Note>

HollowRover is an autonomous physical response unit designed for continuous, unsupervised deployment in residential and assisted-living environments. It runs **ROS2 Humble** on a **D-Robotics RDK X5** compute board — a 4 GB, 8-core Cortex-A55 SoC with a dedicated 10 TOPS neural processing unit — giving the rover the computational headroom to run real-time perception, localization, and decision pipelines simultaneously, without offloading inference to the cloud.

Unlike conventional service robots that respond to explicit commands, HollowRover is **biomarker-driven**: it receives structured alert payloads from LifeSphere's upstream sensing subsystems (BioPulse wearables, EntryGuard perimeter nodes, HollowCore AI engine) and autonomously dispatches physical protocols calibrated to the detected clinical event.

## Localization Architecture

Precise indoor localization is a prerequisite for any autonomous caregiving action — a rover that cannot find its user cannot help them. HollowRover implements a **three-tier localization stack**, each tier serving as a fallback for the one above it.

<Steps>
  <Step title="UWB Precision Localization (Primary)">
    Three **DWM1000** Ultra-Wideband anchors are mounted per registered floor in a non-collinear triangle. The rover carries a fourth DWM1000 tag and continuously measures time-of-flight ranging to all three anchors. A closed-form 2D trilateration solver resolves position to **±10 cm accuracy**, eliminating the left-right ambiguity inherent in 2-anchor systems. Each floor's anchor geometry is stored alongside its SLAM map — on floor transitions, the rover loads the corresponding geometry and map together.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Barometric Floor Detection">
    A **BME688** barometric pressure sensor provides floor-level disambiguation. Each floor stores a pressure baseline at commissioning time. The rover computes the pressure delta relative to the current reading and selects the nearest matching floor entry. This index drives SLAM map and UWB anchor set selection.
  </Step>
  <Step title="BLE RSSI Fallback">
    When UWB signal quality degrades — partial anchor occlusion, transient interference — the rover falls back to **HC-05 BLE RSSI scanning**. It rotates through a set of headings, samples signal strength at each, and navigates toward the heading of maximum received signal. Once UWB quality is re-established, the rover transitions back to trilateration seamlessly. No manual intervention is required.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## SLAM and Navigation

HollowRover runs **SLAM Toolbox** in lifelong mapping mode, maintaining persistent, updateable maps across power cycles. The **STL-19P TOF LiDAR** (12 m range, 5000 Hz scan rate) feeds the occupancy grid. **Nav2** handles global and local planning with a maximum translational speed of 0.3 m/s and a 0.35 m safety inflation radius around obstacles — conservative parameters chosen to protect care recipients from collision.

The drivetrain uses **Ackermann geometry** with machined steering knuckles, ensuring geometrically correct turning arcs without wheel scrub. A spring-loaded rocker suspension with a differential linking bar keeps all four wheels in ground contact over uneven floor transitions.

## Biomarker-Triggered Autonomous Protocols

HollowRover subscribes to structured event payloads published to Firebase by HollowCore. On receipt of a confirmed biomarker event, the rover cancels any active navigation goal, computes a path to the alert origin, and executes the protocol mapped to the event class.

| Event Class | Autonomous Protocol |
|---|---|
| **Medication schedule** | Navigate to user, extend arm, dispense from correct slot, activate pump |
| **Fall detection** | Dispatch to user location, broadcast alert, provide physical support reference |
| **Pre-seizure EEG signature** | Navigate ahead of user, deploy cushioning posture, alert guardian |
| **Cortisol spike (stress)** | Approach user, play de-escalation audio, reduce environmental stimulation |
| **Unregistered face (EntryGuard)** | Arm extends to passive barrier position, alarm activates, guardian notified |

All protocols are **read-only with respect to human contact** by default — the rover positions and informs; escalation requiring physical restraint or forced action requires explicit guardian authorization.

## Multi-Floor Deployment

HollowRover is designed for **multi-floor residential deployment**. Each floor is independently commissioned with:

- Three UWB anchors in a surveyed, non-collinear configuration
- A BME688 pressure baseline reading
- A SLAM Toolbox map built during initial floor traversal

**EntryGuard** perimeter nodes — deployed at doorways and floor transitions — provide zone metadata that allows HollowRover to identify which floor and which zone generated an alert, and to self-position accordingly after a floor transition (via lift, ramp, or manual relocation).

<Note>
  HollowRover does not physically climb stairs. Floor transitions require a lift, ramp, or manual relocation. After relocation, the rover re-establishes its floor context automatically via barometric sensing and reinitializes the appropriate SLAM map and UWB anchor geometry.
</Note>

## 6-DOF Robotic Arm

A six-degree-of-freedom robotic arm — five **MG996R** servos plus one **MG90S** gripper — provides HollowRover with physical manipulation capability. The arm operates in two primary modes:

- **Caregiving assist**: retrieves dropped objects, presents medication and water at accessible height
- **Passive security barrier**: swings to a blocking position across a pathway when an unregistered individual is detected

The arm rests stowed below the LiDAR scan plane during all navigation. It only rises when the rover is stationary in a security-response or dispensing mode — navigation and arm-deployment states are mutually exclusive.
